## Problem

Respiratory-triggered 3D MRCP takes minutes (338 s in this study) and relies on regular breathing. Irregular breathers get motion artifacts.

A breath-hold scan avoids that, but compressed sensing still needs around 20 s of breath-holding and loses SNR at high acceleration. Supervised deep learning would need fully-sampled breath-hold data, which cannot be acquired.

## Approach

Zero-shot self-supervised learning splits the k-space of a single scan into three disjoint sets and trains an unrolled network for that scan alone.

The catch is training time, several hours per scan. The network is split so the first n stages are frozen copies of a pretrained SSDU model and only the last m stages are trained, which cuts the backpropagation depth.

## Implementation

A 13-stage unrolled network with gradient-based data consistency. Breath-hold MRCP used an incoherent sampling pattern at R = 25, acquired in 14 s.

Eleven healthy volunteers were scanned at 3 T and compared with respiratory-triggered parallel imaging (R = 3, 338 s) and compressed sensing. For two of them, 40 s breath-holds at R = 6 were undersampled to R = 25 to compute PSNR.

## Results

In a reader study with three MRI experts, zero-shot reconstruction scored significantly higher than CS for overall quality, noise and aliasing, and came close to successful respiratory-triggered scans.

Freezing 12 stages lowered PSNR only from 38.25 dB to 37.67 dB, while average training time across the 11 volunteers fell from 136 min to 20 min, 6.7× faster.
