## Problem

Respiratory-triggered 3D MRCP takes minutes (338 s here) and requires regular breathing. Irregular breathing may cause artifacts.

Breath-holding reduces motion, but a CS scan may still require about 20 s, and high acceleration can lower SNR. Fully sampled breath-hold MRCP is impractical, limiting training with fully sampled targets.

## Approach

Zero-shot learning splits the acquired k-space into three disjoint sets and trains an unrolled network for that scan.

Training takes hours per scan. To reduce backpropagation depth, the first n stages use frozen weights from a pretrained SSDU model, while only the last m stages are trained.

## Implementation

A 13-stage unrolled network used gradient-based data consistency. MRCP was acquired in 14 s at R = 25 with incoherent sampling.

Eleven healthy volunteers were scanned at 3 T. Comparators were respiratory-triggered PI (R = 3, 338 s) and CS. For PSNR, two 40-s breath-hold scans at R = 6 were retrospectively undersampled to R = 25.

## Results

Three MRI experts rated zero-shot reconstructions significantly higher than CS for quality, noise, and aliasing. Quality was comparable to respiratory-triggered scans with regular breathing.

Freezing 12 stages reduced PSNR from 38.25 to 37.67 dB; mean training time across 11 volunteers fell from about 136 to 20 min.
